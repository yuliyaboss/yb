# 40-PAGE STORYBOARD
## *The World Through My Eyes* — 40 Magical Coloring Pages for Curious Little Minds

### The spine of the book

The five sections are not five themes. They are five stages of one journey, and the book
only works if a child feels the pull from one to the next:

| | Section | Stage | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–8 | Ordinary Things Become Magical | **Real world** | the everyday starts to shimmer |
| 9–16 | Little Adventures | **Imagination** | they step inside and go somewhere |
| 17–24 | What If Animals Could… | **Adventure** | the rules of the world bend |
| 25–32 | Dream Worlds | **Wonder** | the furthest point from home |
| 33–40 | The World Is Full of Wonder | **Everyday world** | back home, seeing differently |

**The device that holds Section 1 together:** never split the ordinary and the magical
across two pages. On one single page the cardboard box *has* fins and a porthole and the
stars are already behind it. The transformation is happening, not remembered.

**The device that closes the book:** page 37 returns to the puddle from page 1. Same
puddle, same boots, and the little sailboat is still out there on it. A child who has
colored 36 pages in between understands exactly what that means without being told.

**Structural note.** Five of the 40 pages (16, 18, 31, 38, 39) invite the child to add
their own drawing as well as color. They are still complete coloring pages — nothing is
left blank that a child would be disappointed to find blank.

# SECTION 1 — ORDINARY THINGS BECOME MAGICAL
*Pages 1–8 · the real world, beginning to shimmer*

---

### Page 1 — THE PUDDLE THAT WAS AN OCEAN

**Scene.** Mia stands at the edge of a rain puddle in her boots. Inside the puddle's
outline the water is a whole sea: rolling waves, a small sailboat, a whale spouting. Leo
crouches on the far side with one hand in the water. Waffles is already standing *in* it,
unimpressed. Pip watches from a lamppost above.

**Characters.** Mia, Leo, Waffles, Pip. (Momo appears from page 2 — the full cast assembles
across the opening.)

**Main action.** Mia points down into the puddle; Leo reaches in to touch the little boat.

**Environment.** A quiet street after rain. Pavement, a lamppost, two low rooftops behind.

**Composition.** The puddle is a wide horizontal oval filling the lower third, its rim on
the ground line at y≈940. Mia stands left at scale 1.35, Leo crouches right. The lamppost
carries the composition up to y≈300 so the page does not bottom-crowd. Sky left clear.

**Major coloring areas.** The puddle interior (the biggest single area in the book), the
sailboat's two sails, the whale's body, Mia's pinafore, both pairs of boots.

**Secondary details (6).** Lamppost, two rooftops, three wave lines, whale spout, Pip,
one drifting cloud.

**Why it belongs.** It is the thesis of the entire book in one image, and it is the page a
browsing parent will see first on Amazon's Look Inside. It teaches the reader the rule —
look again, it is more than it appears — before asking them to believe it anywhere else.

**Difficulty.** Medium · 11 elements.

**Builds from.** `scene_street` · `pond` (as the puddle rim) · `waves` · `sailboat` ·
`whale_spout` · `lamppost` · `kid_point` · `kid_stand(pose="down")` · `dachshund` ·
`bird_side`

### Page 2 — THE BOX THAT WAS A ROCKET

**Scene.** A cardboard box sits in the middle of a living-room rug, and it has grown fins,
a nose cone and a round porthole. Leo's head and both arms stick out of the top, gripping
a drawn-on control panel. Mia stands beside it holding up a hand-drawn countdown sign.
Momo's head and one paw emerge from a smaller box behind — her first appearance.

**Characters.** Leo, Mia, Momo.

**Main action.** Leo launches; Mia counts down.

**Environment.** Living room — rug, couch arm at the left edge, a window showing stars even
though it is daytime. That window is the page's joke and its whole meaning.

**Composition.** Rocket-box centred, 320 px tall, its fins on the rug line at y≈930. The
window at upper right (y 220–420) fills the middle band. Couch arm anchors the left.

**Major coloring areas.** Box body, the two fins, nose cone, porthole, rug, window sky.

**Secondary details (7).** Countdown sign, control panel dials, Momo's box, couch arm,
three stars in the window, tape strips on the box corners.

**Why it belongs.** The second-best-known imagination trope in childhood, and the first
page where a character physically climbs *inside* the transformation rather than looking
at it. Sets up Leo as the builder.

**Difficulty.** Medium · 12 elements.

**Builds from.** `rug` · `couch` · `window` · `rrect` (box) · `rocket` (fins and cone,
scaled) · `kid_stand` · `cat_sitting` (partial, occluded) · `star`

### Page 3 — THE BLANKET THAT WAS A CASTLE

**Scene.** A blanket thrown over two chairs, and rising out of its folds: two cone-roofed
towers with flags, an arched doorway where the blanket gaps. Mia stands in the doorway
wearing a paper crown. Waffles lies across the entrance like a very long, very bored
drawbridge.

**Characters.** Mia, Waffles, Momo (one eye and two ears visible under the blanket hem).

**Main action.** Mia holds the blanket-door open and looks out at the reader.

**Environment.** A corner of a bedroom. Floorboards, one chair leg visible at each side.

**Composition.** The blanket-castle is a broad triangular mass, base y≈935, apex flags at
y≈380. Mia at centre, 260 px. Waffles horizontal across the bottom at y≈900, his length
spanning almost half the page width — the gag needs the room.

**Major coloring areas.** Blanket body, two tower roofs, the arch, Mia's crown, Waffles'
saddle patch.

**Secondary details (5).** Two flags, chair legs, floorboard lines, Momo's ears, one
tassel on the blanket corner.

**Why it belongs.** The gentlest page in Section 1 and the first Easy page — placed third
so a child who found page 2 busy gets an immediate win. Also the page that establishes
Waffles' running joke.

**Difficulty.** Easy · 9 elements.

**Builds from.** `cushion_fort` · `castle_small` (towers only, upper portion) · `crown` ·
`kid_stand` · `dachshund` · `cat_sitting` (ears only)

### Page 4 — THE SOFA THAT WAS A MOUNTAIN

**Scene.** Leo is halfway up the arm of the sofa, one leg braced, both arms reaching for
the summit cushion. Behind and above him a mountain range echoes the sofa's exact
silhouette, rising into the upper half of the page. Waffles sits at the base beside a tiny
flag marked base camp.

**Characters.** Leo, Waffles, Pip (circling near the peak).

**Main action.** Leo climbs.

**Environment.** Living room becoming alpine. The rug below is the valley floor.

**Composition.** Sofa across the lower third, y 700–930. The mountain range behind spans
y 300–700 and repeats the sofa's profile at larger scale — the rhyme between the two
shapes is the whole idea and must survive to final art. Leo at the sofa arm, 250 px.

**Major coloring areas.** Sofa body, two cushions, three mountain faces, the rug.

**Secondary details (6).** Base-camp flag, Waffles, Pip, two snow lines on the peaks,
a rope over Leo's shoulder.

**Why it belongs.** Large simple shapes make this one of the most colorable pages in the
book, and the visual rhyme between furniture and mountain is the clearest statement of the
book's core idea anywhere in Section 1.

**Difficulty.** Easy · 8 elements.

**Builds from.** `couch` · `mountain_range` · `rug` · `kid_reach` · `dachshund` ·
`bird_side` · `wooden_sign` (as the base-camp flag)

### Page 5 — THE STICK THAT WAS A MAGIC WAND

**Scene.** Mia holds a crooked garden stick aloft, and sparkles are pouring off its tip and
drifting across the whole upper page. Beside her Waffles has found a stick of his own,
which he is carrying proudly, and nothing whatsoever is happening at his end.

**Characters.** Mia, Waffles, Pip.

**Main action.** Mia casts; Waffles carries.

**Environment.** A garden — flower bed along the bottom, one round tree at the right.

**Composition.** Mia left of centre at 280 px, right arm raised high so the wand tip lands
at y≈330. Sparkle trail sweeps diagonally to the upper right. Tree at right y 400–930 holds
the tall side. Waffles bottom-left, in his own pool of empty ground.

**Major coloring areas.** Mia's pinafore, the tree canopy, the flower bed, Waffles' patch.

**Secondary details (7).** Nine sparkles of two sizes, the two sticks, three flowers,
grass tufts, Pip on a branch.

**Why it belongs.** The book's first page of pure imagination with no supporting prop —
nothing about the stick has physically changed. And the Waffles gag lands the difference
between believing and not: same stick, different child.

**Difficulty.** Medium · 12 elements.

**Builds from.** `scene_meadow` · `garden_strip` · `tree_round` · `wand` · `sparkle` ·
`kid_stand(pose="wave")` · `dachshund` · `bird_side`

### Page 6 — THE LAUNDRY BASKET THAT WAS A BOAT

**Scene.** Mia sits inside a laundry basket that is riding a sea of spilled washing. A sock
is rigged as a sail on a broom-handle mast. Waffles stands at the prow like a figurehead.
Wave lines run out across the floor in every direction.

**Characters.** Mia, Waffles.

**Main action.** Mia steers with a wooden spoon; Waffles looks out to sea.

**Environment.** A laundry room. Washing machine at the left, a shelf above it.

**Composition.** Basket centred, 300 px wide, waterline at y≈880. The mast carries to
y≈340. Washing machine anchors the left at y 600–930, and its round door echoes the
porthole from page 2.

**Major coloring areas.** Basket weave, the sock sail, washing-machine door, three large
wave shapes.

**Secondary details (6).** Spoon, mast, shelf with two bottles, scattered socks, Waffles'
collar tag, a bubble trail.

**Why it belongs.** Domestic, small, and funny — an Easy page placed between two Mediums,
and one of the few settings a coloring book never uses. It also gives Waffles his best
single pose in Section 1.

**Difficulty.** Easy · 9 elements.

**Builds from.** `basket` · `sock` · `waves` · `rrect` (machine) · `kid_sitting` ·
`dachshund`

### Page 7 — THE TUBE THAT WAS A TELESCOPE

**Scene.** Night. Leo kneels on a window seat with a cardboard tube pressed to one eye,
aimed out of the window. Through the glass: a huge moon with craters, a ringed planet, a
scatter of stars. Momo sits on the sill in silhouette, tail curled, watching the same sky.

**Characters.** Leo, Momo, Mia (asleep under a blanket at the left edge, only her bob and
one hand showing).

**Main action.** Leo looks; Momo looks with him.

**Environment.** A bedroom window at night.

**Composition.** The window is the dominant shape — a tall rounded rectangle from y 200 to
y 760, which single-handedly solves the vertical span. Leo kneels below right. Momo on the
sill at the window's lower edge, breaking the frame line so she does not read as a sticker.

**Major coloring areas.** Moon disc, ringed planet, window frame, Leo's tee, the cushion.

**Secondary details (7).** Cardboard tube, eleven stars at three sizes, window-seat
cushion, curtain edge, Momo's stripes, Mia's blanket, one crater cluster.

**Why it belongs.** The quiet page of the section, and the one that plants space so that
Dream Worlds in Section 4 feels earned rather than arbitrary. Also the first appearance of
night, which the book will return to four more times.

**Difficulty.** Medium · 13 elements.

**Builds from.** `window` · `moon` · `planet_ringed` · `star_field` · `telescope` (barrel
geometry, scaled to a tube) · `kid_stand(pose="down")` · `cat_sitting` · `bed`

### Page 8 — THE UMBRELLA THAT WAS A HOT-AIR BALLOON

**Scene.** Mia and Leo hang from the handle of an open umbrella, rising above the rooftops
of their town. The umbrella has become a balloon envelope with a wicker basket below it,
caught exactly halfway between both things. Waffles hangs from the basket rim by his teeth,
body dangling at full absurd length. Pip flies level with them. Momo watches from a chimney
far below.

**Characters.** All five.

**Main action.** They lift off.

**Environment.** Sky above the town. Rooftops, chimneys and a church spire fill the lower
quarter; clouds at three heights.

**Composition.** Balloon-umbrella centred at y 200–560, basket at y 560–680, Waffles
hanging to y≈800. The town skyline runs along y 850–940, which is unusually low — this is
the one page in the book where the composition is deliberately *top*-weighted, because the
whole subject is leaving the ground.

**Major coloring areas.** Umbrella canopy in six panels, basket weave, three rooftops,
two large clouds.

**Secondary details (8).** Church spire, four chimneys, Momo on one of them, Pip, three
ropes, the umbrella's hook handle, two birds in the distance, one weather vane.

**Why it belongs.** The section's climax and the hinge of the whole book — the last page
of the real world and the first page of flight. Every character appears, which signals a
turn. The umbrella that is also a balloon, drawn as one object refusing to be either, is
the most sophisticated image in Section 1 and deserves the closing slot.

**Difficulty.** Challenging · 16 elements.

**Builds from.** `hot_air_balloon` · `skyline` · `cloud` · `kid_stand` ×2 (hanging pose
via `kid_reach` mirrored) · `dachshund` · `cat_sitting` · `bird_side` · `village_house`

# SECTION 2 — LITTLE ADVENTURES
*Pages 9–16 · inside the imagination now, and going somewhere*

---

### Page 9 — THE BACKYARD EXPEDITION

**Scene.** Mia leads with a hand-drawn map held open in both hands; Leo follows with a
saucepan helmet and a backpack far too large for him. The garden around them has gone
jungle: oversized leaves, a dense tree line, one suspiciously large flower. Waffles trots
ahead nose-down on the trail. Pip scouts from a high branch.

**Characters.** Mia, Leo, Waffles, Pip.

**Main action.** The expedition sets off along a winding path.

**Environment.** A back garden reading as jungle. A dashed path curves from the bottom
edge into the tree line.

**Composition.** Path enters bottom-left, curves to a vanishing point at y≈700 centre-right.
Forest border spans y 450–800 behind. Mia and Leo staggered in depth — Mia at 1.35, Leo at
1.20 and slightly behind, which is how the page shows distance without perspective tricks.

**Major coloring areas.** Four oversized leaves, the backpack, the map, Mia's pinafore,
the giant flower.

**Secondary details (7).** Saucepan helmet, dashed path, Waffles, Pip, three grass tufts,
a butterfly, one mushroom.

**Why it belongs.** The section opener has to say *we are going somewhere now*. A map, a
path leaving the frame and a character already ahead of the others say it faster than any
caption could.

**Difficulty.** Medium · 13 elements.

**Builds from.** `scene_meadow` · `forest_border` · `leaf` ×4 · `sunflower` (oversized) ·
`mushroom` · `butterfly` · `kid_carry` · `kid_point` · `dachshund` · `bird_side`

### Page 10 — THE TREEHOUSE LOOKOUT

**Scene.** A treehouse sitting in the fork of a big tree, ladder leaning against the trunk.
Leo leans out of the window with the cardboard tube from page 7, now a spyglass. Mia is
halfway up the ladder. Pip perches on the roof ridge. Below, Waffles has both front paws on
the bottom rung and no idea what to do next.

**Characters.** Leo, Mia, Waffles, Pip.

**Main action.** Climbing up to look out.

**Environment.** The big garden tree, ground line at y≈940, grass and two bushes.

**Composition.** Trunk rises at centre-left from y 940 to the canopy at y 300. The house
sits on the branch fork at y 480–700, below the foliage, with visible plank supports — a
structure floating inside a leaf blob is the classic failure here. The ladder leans clear of
the trunk, never overlapping it.

**Major coloring areas.** Canopy, treehouse walls, roof, trunk, two bushes.

**Secondary details (6).** Ladder rungs, spyglass, a rope with a bucket, Pip, Waffles,
a knothole.

**Why it belongs.** Height as a reward for effort — the first page where the characters
work for their vantage point. The canopy also gives the book its largest single green mass,
which is a satisfying thing to hand a child.

**Difficulty.** Medium · 12 elements.

**Builds from.** `treehouse` · `tree_round` (canopy) · `kid_stand` · `kid_reach` ·
`dachshund` · `bird_side` · `grass_tuft`

### Page 11 — CROSSING THE GREAT PUDDLE

**Scene.** A pond with five stepping stones across it. Mia is mid-stride between stones
three and four, arms out for balance. Waffles has decided to be the bridge instead and is
stretched between two stones at full length, with Momo walking across his back.

**Characters.** Mia, Waffles, Momo, Pip.

**Main action.** Crossing — two different solutions to the same problem, side by side.

**Environment.** A pond in the meadow. Reeds at both banks, one duck.

**Composition.** Pond as a wide ellipse, rim on the ground line at y≈900. Stones in a
staggered line. Mia at 1.30 centre. Waffles spans the lower right at full 1.6 length, which
is the single clearest statement of his proportions in the book.

**Major coloring areas.** Pond water, five stones, the duck, Mia's dress, Waffles' patch.

**Secondary details (6).** Reeds both sides, ripple arcs, Momo, Pip on a reed, two lily
pads, a dragonfly.

**Why it belongs.** An Easy page after two Mediums, and the funniest single image in
Section 2. The joke also does real work: it shows Waffles solving a problem by being
exactly what he is, which is the section's quiet argument.

**Difficulty.** Easy · 10 elements.

**Builds from.** `pond` · `stones` · `duck` · `kid_stand` · `dachshund` · `cat_sitting` ·
`bird_side` · `grass_tuft`

### Page 12 — THE SANDBOX DESERT

**Scene.** A sandbox that has become a desert. Leo digs with a spade, sand flying; Mia
kneels beside a half-buried wooden chest with its lid just breaking the surface. A cactus
stands at the far corner of the frame. Waffles has dug his own hole and is in it up to the
shoulders.

**Characters.** Leo, Mia, Waffles.

**Main action.** Digging for treasure.

**Environment.** A sandbox, its wooden frame reading as the horizon of a small desert. Sun
high right, two distant dunes.

**Composition.** The sandbox frame runs across y 780–940 and the dunes continue its line
outward, so the box edge and the desert horizon are the same stroke. Leo standing at 1.30,
Mia kneeling — the kneel handled as an implied pose, skirt bell plus knee bumps, never
articulated folded legs.

**Major coloring areas.** Sand field, the chest, cactus, sandbox frame boards, two dunes.

**Secondary details (7).** Spade, bucket, flying sand clods, Waffles' hole, three sand
ripples, sun, one lizard.

**Why it belongs.** A different flavour of adventure — downward instead of onward — and the
treasure chest is the first object in the book that is genuinely mysterious. It is also the
page where the imagined world keeps the shape of the real object most visibly.

**Difficulty.** Medium · 13 elements.

**Builds from.** `sandbox` · `cactus` · `hill` ×2 (dunes) · `dirt_hole` · `sun` ·
`kid_stand(pose="down")` · `kid_sitting` · `dachshund`

### Page 13 — THE KITE THAT PULLED US UP

**Scene.** A big diamond kite high in the upper page, string running diagonally down to
Mia, whose heels are just off the ground. Leo has both arms around her waist, holding on,
also rising. Their hair blows one way; a wind swirl sweeps across the sky.

**Characters.** Mia, Leo, Pip.

**Main action.** The kite lifts them both.

**Environment.** Open meadow, low hill behind, tall grass bending in the wind.

**Composition.** Kite at upper right, y 220–400 — it holds the top of the page alone, so
the midground hill at y 700–860 is essential to keep the connected mass reaching y≈450.
The string is a single clean diagonal and the page's strongest line.

**Major coloring areas.** Kite face in four panels, the hill, both children, three tail
bows.

**Secondary details (6).** Wind swirl, five grass tufts, Pip flying, kite string, two
clouds, one dandelion seed head.

**Why it belongs.** Physical joy with almost nothing in it — a deliberately Easy page after
the busy sandbox, and the moment the section's rising motion becomes literal. It also sets
the visual key for the cloud pages in Section 4.

**Difficulty.** Easy · 9 elements.

**Builds from.** `scene_meadow` · `kite` · `wind_swirl` · `hill` · `kid_stand` ·
`kids_holding_hands` (adapted) · `bird_side` · `grass_tuft`

### Page 14 — CAMPFIRE UNDER THE FIRST STAR

**Scene.** Evening. A tent behind, a ring of stones with a fire in it, two log seats. Mia
and Leo sit toasting marshmallows on long sticks. Waffles lies full-length along one log
like a draught excluder. Momo is a pair of eyes just outside the firelight. Pip is on the
tent's pole flag. One single star above.

**Characters.** All five.

**Main action.** Toasting marshmallows; the first star comes out.

**Environment.** A camp in the garden at dusk. Tree line behind at low height.

**Composition.** Tent at left, y 560–940. Fire at centre on the ground line. The children
face each other across it, which gives the page its only symmetrical moment in the whole
book. Star at upper right, alone in a lot of white space — restraint is the point.

**Major coloring areas.** Tent body, flame tongues, two logs, both children, the fire ring
stones.

**Secondary details (7).** Two marshmallow sticks, s'more on a plate, Waffles, Momo's eyes,
Pip, flag, three tree silhouettes.

**Why it belongs.** The book's rest stop. Section 2 has been pushing outward and upward for
five pages; this one stops, sits down and is warm. Every book for this age needs a page
that does nothing except feel safe, and it should arrive just before the halfway mark.

**Difficulty.** Easy · 10 elements.

**Builds from.** `tent` · `campfire` · `log_seat` ×2 · `marsh_stick` · `smore` ·
`forest_border` · `kid_sitting` ×2 · `dachshund` · `bird_side` · `star`

### Page 15 — THE MIDNIGHT SNACK QUEST

**Scene.** A kitchen at night. Leo is on tiptoe on a chair, reaching for a biscuit tin on
the high shelf. Mia holds the chair steady with one hand and a torch with the other, its
beam a clean cone across the cupboards. Momo is already on the counter and has already got
there first. Waffles waits below with total faith.

**Characters.** Leo, Mia, Momo, Waffles.

**Main action.** The reach.

**Environment.** Kitchen at night — counter, cupboards, shelf, window with the moon.

**Composition.** Cupboard run fills y 300–700 across the upper half, which is what makes
this an indoor page that still spans. Leo's reaching hand at y≈380, the tin just above it.
Torch beam is a long triangle, and it is the page's only diagonal.

**Major coloring areas.** Cupboard doors, the biscuit tin, torch beam, counter front,
moon in the window.

**Secondary details (7).** Chair, four jars on the counter, Momo, Waffles, kettle,
floor tiles, a mug.

**Why it belongs.** Mild transgression is delicious at five. It is also the last page of
pure domestic reality before the animal section rewrites the rules, and the torch beam
quietly rehearses the light-through-window motif that closes the book on page 33.

**Difficulty.** Medium · 13 elements.

**Builds from.** `window` · `moon` · `bookshelf` (as the cupboard run) · `table` ·
`teapot` · `kid_reach` · `kid_stand` · `cat_sitting` · `dachshund`

### Page 16 — THE MAP WE DREW OURSELVES

**Scene.** A large sheet of paper spread across the whole page, its corners weighted with
a stone, a mug, a shoe and Momo. On the paper: a partly drawn treasure map — a coastline, a
forest of small trees, three landmarks, a dashed route that runs for a while and then stops
in open space with a large question mark. Mia and Leo lie on their stomachs at the bottom
edge, drawing.

**Characters.** Mia, Leo, Momo.

**Main action.** They are drawing the map — and the child finishes it.

**Environment.** The map itself is the environment. Floorboards visible at the margins.

**Composition.** Map fills y 180–900 as a slightly rotated rectangle. The route starts at
a compass rose bottom-left, passes three landmarks and stops at y≈420 with generous empty
paper beyond. The children's heads and shoulders break the bottom edge of the map, which
keeps them part of the scene rather than framing it.

**Major coloring areas.** Landmark drawings (mountain, tree, tower), compass rose, the
four corner weights, the map's torn edge.

**Secondary details (6).** Dashed route, question mark, coastline, two pencils, Momo,
floorboards.

**Why it belongs.** The section's closer and the book's first invitation to add rather than
fill in. Section 2 has been about going somewhere; it ends by handing the child the
decision about where. The blank half of the map is the point, and it should look like
paper, not like an unfinished page.

**Difficulty.** Easy · 10 elements · **activity page** (`layout="activity"`).

**Builds from.** `rrect` (map) · `mountain` · `tree_pine` · `castle_small` (small
landmark) · `dotline` · `stones` · `cat_sitting` · `kid_sitting` ×2 · `star` (compass)

# SECTION 3 — WHAT IF ANIMALS COULD…
*Pages 17–24 · the rules of the world start to bend*

Each page in this section asks its question in the title and answers it in the picture.
Big animal bodies mean big colorable areas, so this is the most forgiving section for the
youngest readers even though it sits in the middle of the book.

---

### Page 17 — WHAT IF WAFFLES COULD FLY?

**Scene.** Waffles airborne above the rooftops, with a pair of paper-plane wings taped
along his impossible length. Ears streaming backwards. Pip flies alongside in formation,
clearly the flight instructor. Below, Leo stands on the garden wall with both arms up,
having just let go.

**Characters.** Waffles, Pip, Leo.

**Main action.** Waffles flies, badly and joyfully.

**Environment.** Above the back garden. Rooftops, treetops and a washing line below.

**Composition.** Waffles occupies the upper half on a shallow diagonal, y 260–500, at full
length — the longest single shape in the book. Leo small on the wall at y 780. Washing line
strung across the lower third gives the mid-band something to do.

**Major coloring areas.** Waffles' body and saddle patch, both paper wings, two rooftops,
three hanging shirts on the line.

**Secondary details (6).** Tape strips, Pip, the wall, two treetops, four clouds, Leo.

**Why it belongs.** The section opens with the character a child already loves doing the
one thing he obviously cannot. Starting with Waffles rather than a generic animal also
keeps the cast at the centre of the section instead of letting it drift into a zoo.

**Difficulty.** Medium · 12 elements.

**Builds from.** `dachshund` · `paper_plane` (wings, scaled) · `bird_side` · `skyline` ·
`cloud` · `kid_jump` · `sock` ×3 (washing)

---

### Page 18 — WHAT IF MOMO COULD TALK?

**Scene.** Momo sits square in the middle of a sunlit windowsill, facing the reader
directly — the only front-on pose in the book. Above her, a large empty speech bubble. A
knocked-over plant pot behind her, soil spilled, and a look of complete innocence.

**Characters.** Momo, Pip (outside on the window ledge, looking in).

**Main action.** Momo says something. The child decides what.

**Environment.** A windowsill, the garden visible through the glass behind.

**Composition.** The speech bubble is the largest object on the page, y 220–500, tail
pointing down to Momo's head but stopping well clear of it. Momo centred at y 560–860.
Window frame divides the background into four clean panes.

**Major coloring areas.** The speech bubble interior (deliberately the biggest empty area
in the book), Momo's body and stripes, the plant pot, four window panes.

**Secondary details (5).** Spilled soil, plant leaves, Pip, sill edge, curtain corner.

# SECTION 4 — DREAM WORLDS
*Pages 25–32 · the furthest point from home*

The book's visual register changes here. Night, floating, no ground line on four of the
eight pages. Where earlier sections built upward from a ground, these pages are composed
around a centre — which is what makes them feel weightless without needing to explain it.

# SECTION 5 — THE WORLD IS FULL OF WONDER
*Pages 33–40 · home again, and nothing looks the way it did on page 1*

No magic is added in this section. Every page is something a child could genuinely walk
past this afternoon. That is the entire point: after thirty-two pages of transformation,
the reader has been taught to do the transforming themselves, and the book stops doing it
for them.

### Page 33 — MORNING LIGHT ON THE KITCHEN FLOOR

**Scene.** A rectangle of sunlight lying across the kitchen floor, drawn as a clean
outlined shape with the window's cross-bars printed inside it. Momo is lying in the middle
of it with her eyes shut. Waffles has arrived to lie in it too and there is not enough room.
Mia sits on the floor in the corner of the light with a cup.

**Characters.** Mia, Momo, Waffles.

**Main action.** Nothing. Three creatures share a patch of sun.

**Environment.** Kitchen floor, early morning. Table legs, a chair, the window above.

**Composition.** The light patch is a long parallelogram running from the window at upper
right down to the lower left, y 500–900, and it is the page's main shape. Window at y

**Secondary details (5).** Table legs, chair, floor tiles as four large squares, the

**Why it belongs.** The section opens by proving its own claim. A patch of light on the

frame, so the bird is small but unmissable. Music notes trail diagonally into the room from
upper right to lower left, which is the only thing moving on the page. Lamp and picture
frame hold the left side.

**Major coloring areas.** Window frame and panes, curtain, the tree branch, lamp shade,
Mia's pinafore.

**Secondary details (7).** Nine music notes at two sizes, Pip, Momo, picture frame, three
leaves, the sill, a plant pot.

**Why it belongs.** The only page in the book about sound, saved until almost the end so it
lands as something new rather than as a gimmick. It also pays off Pip's music-note motif
from the character bible — thirty-five pages of setup for one small moment.

**Difficulty.** Easy · 10 elements.

**Builds from.** `window` · `bird_side` · `music_note` ×9 · `floor_lamp` ·
`wall_picture` · `cat_sitting` · `kid_stand` · `potted_plant` · `leaf`

### Page 37 — THE PUDDLE, AGAIN

**Scene.** The same street corner as page 1. The same puddle, the same lamppost, the same
two rooftops. The rain has stopped properly now and the puddle is only reflecting the sky
and the lamppost — no waves, no whale. Except right out in the middle of it, small and
clear, the little sailboat is still there. Mia stands looking at it with her hands behind
her back. Leo is crouched beside her again. Waffles is standing in it again. Momo has come
too, this time. Pip is on the same lamppost arm.

**Characters.** All five.

**Main action.** Looking at the puddle a second time, on purpose.

**Environment.** Identical to page 1 — this only works if it is genuinely the same drawing
underneath, with elements removed rather than a new scene.

**Composition.** Deliberately near-identical to page 1: same puddle oval on the ground line,
same lamppost carrying the top, Mia left and Leo right. The differences are all subtractive
— the waves and whale are gone, the reflection lines are calm — plus one addition, Momo, in
the space where the whale used to be.

**Major coloring areas.** Puddle interior (now mostly empty — the largest single open area
in the book), the reflected lamppost, the sailboat, Mia's pinafore, both rooftops.

**Secondary details (6).** Reflection lines, the lamppost, Momo, Pip, two clouds, Waffles'
ripples.

**Why it belongs.** This is the page the whole book exists to arrive at. A child who colored
page 1 forty minutes or four weeks ago will recognise it immediately, spot the boat, and
understand the entire argument of the book without a single word of explanation. The empty
space where the whale was is not a loss — it is the room left for them.

**Difficulty.** Medium · 11 elements.

**Builds from.** identical call stack to page 1, minus `waves` and `whale_spout`, plus
`cat_sitting` — this page should be built by copying page 1's builder function and deleting
from it.

### Page 38 — FIND THE WONDER

**Scene.** A busy ordinary street on a Saturday — shops, a bus stop, a market stall, a dog
walker, a cyclist, a pigeon on a bin, a child with a balloon. Nothing magical anywhere. But
hidden in it: eleven things from earlier in the book. The sailboat is in a shop window. The
lion is on a poster. A fairy door is at the base of a lamppost. The dream machine's funnel
is in a skip.

**Characters.** All five, plus a street's worth of extras.

**Main action.** Searching. A checklist runs down one side of the page in small colorable
boxes.

**Environment.** A town street, fully populated. The book's densest page.

**Composition.** Street kit with the road band across the lower third, shopfronts along
y 400–800, skyline above. The checklist column occupies the right-hand 110 px as a series
of small framed icons, kept clear of the art so it never competes.

**Major coloring areas.** Three shopfronts, the bus, the market awning, the balloon,
eleven checklist icons.

**Secondary details (8).** Bus stop, bin and pigeon, cyclist, lamppost with fairy door,
skip, poster, shop window, the cast dispersed through the crowd.

**Why it belongs.** The find-the-thing page, positioned near the end where it works as a
review of everything the child has colored. It rewards having done the book in order and
gives the whole thing a second life — this is the page a child returns to alone.

**Difficulty.** Challenging · 18 elements · **activity page** (`layout="activity"`).

**Builds from.** `scene_street` · `skyline` · `school_bus` · `lamppost` · `fairy_door` ·
`sailboat` · `lion` (poster) · `balloon` · `bicycle` · `bird_side` · full cast helpers

### Page 39 — DRAW WHAT YOU SEE

**Scene.** Mia and Leo hold up a large empty picture frame between them, turned to face the
reader. Everything inside the frame is blank paper. Around the outside: their own gallery —
six small framed drawings hung on the wall behind them, each one a thumbnail of a page from
earlier in the book. Momo sits on the frame's lower edge, which is exactly the sort of thing
that would ruin a real painting.

**Characters.** Mia, Leo, Momo.

**Main action.** They hand the frame to the reader.

**Environment.** A wall of a room, hung as a gallery.

**Composition.** The big frame is centred, y 260–780, and its interior is the single
largest blank area in the book. The six small frames sit around it in a loose ring. Mia and
Leo stand at either side, hands on the frame, heads above its top edge.

**Major coloring areas.** The big frame's moulding, six small framed pictures, both
children, Momo.

**Secondary details (5).** Picture hooks, wall line, a stool, pencil on the floor, one
crooked frame.

**Why it belongs.** The last invitation and the biggest one. Every earlier activity page
asked the child to finish something; this one asks them to start something. Putting the six
thumbnails around it means a child who does not know what to draw has six reminders in
their peripheral vision.

**Difficulty.** Easy · 10 elements · **activity page** (`layout="creative"`).

**Builds from.** `finish_page` (frame mechanics) · `wall_picture` ×6 · `kid_stand` ×2 ·
`cat_sitting` · `rrect`

### Page 40 — THE WORLD THROUGH MY EYES

**Scene.** A great soap bubble floating in the middle of the page, and inside its curve, in
clean small line work, the whole book: the puddle-ocean, the box-rocket, the blanket castle,
the cloud playground, the staircase of stars, the tea party table. Below it the entire cast
stands together looking up — Mia and Leo side by side, Waffles stretched across in front of
them, Momo on Leo's shoulder for once in full view, Pip on the very top of the bubble where
he should not be able to balance. The book's title runs along the bottom in large hollow
letters for coloring.

**Characters.** All five.

**Main action.** Looking up at everything they have seen.

**Environment.** Open, undefined, warm. A suggestion of ground, a few sparkles, nothing
that places it anywhere in particular.

**Composition.** Bubble centred at y 200–640, its interior scenes drawn at small scale with
plenty of white between them so they read as memories rather than clutter. Cast along
y 640–900. Title letters across y 920–990 in outline form, sized so a child can color
inside them.

**Major coloring areas.** The bubble's rim and highlight, six mini-scenes inside it, the
title letters, all five characters.

**Secondary details (7).** Sparkles, a second smaller bubble, ground suggestion, Momo,
Pip, two drifting stars, the Wonder Star hidden in its hardest position of the book.

**Why it belongs.** The closing image has to do two jobs: hold the whole book in one frame,
and leave the reader on the outside of it looking in, the same way they were on page 1. The
bubble does both — everything is in there, it is beautiful, and it is about to pop, which
is exactly what childhood attention is like and exactly why this book was worth making.

**Difficulty.** Challenging · 16 elements.

**Builds from.** `C` (bubble) · miniature calls to the page 1, 2, 3, 26, 24 and 29
builders at reduced scale · `otext` / `word` (title letters) · `kid_stand` ×2 ·
`dachshund` · `cat_sitting` · `bird_side` · `sparkle`

## Build order recommendation

1. **`dachshund()` and `paper_plane()` first** — two new helpers the whole book depends on.
   Freeze them before any page is drawn.
2. **Page 1, then page 37** — build the callback pair together while the geometry is
   identical, not thirty pages apart.
3. **Page 32 early** — the highest-risk page. If the dream machine does not read after two
   attempts, there is time to simplify it rather than ship it weak.
4. Sections in order after that, with `python -m lib.validate` after every page and a full
   `lint_book` duplicate check once Section 3 is complete.
